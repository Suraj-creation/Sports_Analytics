# Model and algorithm evaluation for the badminton pipeline

Historical research snapshot. Later authorized runtime checks of the unchanged
TrackNet model are recorded in the
[phase-2 report](../architecture/full-pipeline-phase-2-report.md); they establish
bounded Torch tensor parity, not model-accuracy improvement or alternative-model
validation. Exporter dependencies remain unavailable in the existing environment.

Snapshot: 2 November 2026. This is source and model-card research, not a benchmark. No weights, packages, or datasets were downloaded; no model, test, video, or GPU was run. Hardware estimates below are qualitative unless the originator's measurement is explicitly identified. The objective is to guide controlled experiments against our existing baseline, not to select a replacement from a leaderboard. See [the external-repository analysis](../../research/EXTERNAL_BADMINTON_REPOSITORIES_ANALYSIS.md) for J/D/V execution paths and [the architecture contract](../architecture/full-pipeline-architecture.md) for evidence, ownership and runtime constraints.

## Decisions

1. Retain the existing TrackNetV3, person/pose, identity and contact baselines pending labelled evaluation. No reviewed result in this document establishes an accuracy improvement on our amateur singles/doubles footage.
2. Prioritize independent labels and interface checks over adding large foundation models. The first practical candidates are an **independent RacketVision BallTrack adapter**, a **complete racket detection-plus-pose adapter**, and **WASB** as a research comparator, all shadow-only until evidence and rights are resolved.
3. Treat the present manifest's declared licenses as inventory, not a commercial clearance. Check code, each exact checkpoint, mirror, training data and video rights separately. In particular, the RacketVision model host has no model card, and OpenMMLab code licensing alone does not settle every pretrained artifact.
4. Do not infer contact from a class box, segmentation mask or predicted trajectory alone. Preserve missing data and unknown hitter/shot classes; use separate contact, attribution and classification measures.

The configured Pascal GPUs do not by themselves establish that every model or modern CUDA/TensorRT build is supported. On the authorized server, record GPU model, VRAM, compute capability, driver, CUDA/FFmpeg versions, Torch/ONNX Runtime versions, provider and model precision for each run; never interrupt unrelated training. Compare cold/warm startup, end-to-end elapsed time and peak device/host memory, not just model forward time. Synchronize device timing where required and include decoding, crop, copy and postprocessing costs. Run one candidate at a time on bounded segments, respecting the existing scheduler and resource limits.

## Experimental sequence and acceptance criteria

1. **Data readiness:** assemble separately reviewed, match-disjoint development and held-out clips for singles and doubles; record usage rights, camera and lighting, frame timestamps/resampling, calibration, adjacent courts, visible/occluded/absent shuttle and four-person labels. Each clip carries a stable fingerprint. Do not tune on held-out clips. Set explicit match-level split, review protocol, metrics, resource budget and minimum acceptance thresholds *before* looking at candidate results.
2. **Baseline:** with unchanged configuration and pinned artifact digests, save predictions and provenance. Measure detector precision/recall, track fragmentation and ID switches, court-coordinate residual on held-out landmarks, shuttle visibility precision/recall and pixel localization, contact precision/recall plus timing error, hitter attribution conditional on matched contact, shot macro-F1 conditional on matched contact, and rally-boundary accuracy. Report counts, missing labels and uncertainty for each metric.
3. **Identity ablation:** compare baseline association with and without the existing re-ID component, then a separately implemented candidate assignment if justified. Keep detector output and calibration fixed. Evaluate player continuity and abstention at teammate crossings, camera cuts and occlusions; report both four-player slot consistency and name correctness rather than near/far agreement.
4. **Shuttle ablation:** compare current 8-frame non-overlap with its supported overlap as one variable, recording timing and memory. Evaluate RacketVision 4-frame and WASB only through distinct validated contracts with fixed preprocessing, no checkpoint swapping. Use one-to-one matching and visibility-stratified false positives/negatives on the same frames; specifically inspect adjacent-court shuttles and duplicated proxy frames.
5. **Contact and racket ablation:** preserve the existing heuristic components, then evaluate each extra cue or racket model in shadow. Report precision/recall and timing on all independently labelled contacts, including those with missing shuttle or pose; separately report individual-player attribution on matched contacts and number abstained. Prevent contact smoothing or trajectory inpainting from manufacturing a hit.
6. **Classification and downstream:** evaluate BST and rules on identical matched contacts and report the unknown rate and per-class support, then report end-to-end results including missed and false contacts. Evaluate singles and doubles independently; a singles-only checkpoint must not emit a doubles result. Score/rally summaries need independently reviewed intervals and point labels, not merely visual plausibility.
7. **Operational gate:** only consider adoption when the preregistered held-out accuracy and false-positive criteria, resource budgets, data/weight rights, failure behavior, reproducibility manifest and rollback baseline are all met. Preserve failed runs, actual source/checkpoint hashes, config, output schema, calibration, timestamp mapping, software versions and device metadata.

## Evidence and legal follow-ups

- Confirm the exact ONNX mirror and published artifact hashes and licenses for every current model. Several current registry entries use `pin-on-first-fetch`; this detects later changes but does not independently authenticate the first download.
- The [OpenMMLab project license](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/blob/main/LICENSE) is a code license. Request or locate authoritative, checkpoint-specific terms for body7 and the HumanArt-trained YOLOX ONNX export before commercial redistribution; the publisher's [model guide](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/blob/main/docs/en/user_guides/inference.md) establishes usage, not blanket dataset/weight clearance.
- [Ultralytics licensing](https://www.ultralytics.com/license) describes AGPL and enterprise options for its code and models. J or V's use of Ultralytics must not be treated as interchangeable with the locally selected non-Ultralytics adapters or as permission to redistribute their assets.
- A checkpoint hosted under a permissively licensed source repository is not necessarily covered by the same express terms. For [RacketVision model files](https://huggingface.co/linfeng302/RacketVision-Models), the host currently supplies no model card. Verify both stage weights, their creators' terms and footage provenance before deployment.
- Keep the current server environment intact. This document is based on code and primary-source inspection only; there are **no** newly measured model, GPU, or real-video results in this report.

**Checkpoint safety:** the RacketVision [checkpoint listing](https://huggingface.co/linfeng302/RacketVision-Models/tree/main/checkpoints) labels all six hosted files, including BallTrack and racket pose, “Unsafe” and lists pickle imports. This is a hosting-service scan warning, not proof of malicious content. Do not blindly deserialize them. Any future experiment requires trusted provenance and independent digest verification, a supported restricted loader or a separately authorized isolated conversion procedure, and inspection of the resulting artifact. A digest pinned on first fetch cannot establish origin by itself.

**Optional video-language review:** [Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) is an available multimodal candidate whose model card declares Apache-2.0; its [official implementation](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL) accepts sampled video. Consider it only as a human-review search or narrative aid, with footage/privacy rights, revision/hash and model-specific terms checked. Video sampling can skip short contacts and generated descriptions can be wrong; it must never directly create authoritative hits, scores, shot classes or physical measurements. Real inference memory, throughput and Pascal compatibility are unknown. No model was downloaded or tested.

**Existing local study, not a new benchmark:** [BST adapter development notes](../testing/bst-adapter-20261029.md) report a prior 0044 comparison on provider-refined hit anchors with 9 matched predictions, 19 false positives and 32 misses. The notes report that on the repaired candidate BST did not improve the compared rules' known-family F1 and assigned known classes to more false contacts; the held-out recording was reserved. Those anchors are not independently verified physical contacts. The report supports the current shadow-only choice, not a fresh accuracy claim or evidence of performance on doubles.

**Loader and cache caveat:** `TrackNetV3.__init__` calls `torch.load(..., weights_only=False)`; checkpoint safety therefore depends on a trusted source and artifact. At the baseline revision its ONNX filename included sequence length and background mode but not checkpoint digest. This branch adds checkpoint- and exporter-specific naming and atomic publication; the new tests and runtime behavior have not been executed. Existing keyed ONNX graphs are not independently checksummed, the fingerprint does not cover the entire toolchain, and TensorRT provider-engine cache invalidation remains a separate unresolved issue.
