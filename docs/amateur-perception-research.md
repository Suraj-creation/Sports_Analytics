# Amateur badminton perception: research and implementation choices

Research snapshot: 28 October 2026. Upstream benchmarks and theoretical memory estimates are not measurements on this project.

## Local constraints and evidence

The server has two GTX 1080 Ti Pascal GPUs, each reporting 11,264 MiB, not an RTX 4000 Ada. A prior inspection found an existing workload on GPU 0; do not interrupt it. The devices do not automatically pool their memory. The configured environment reported Python 3.12.12, PyTorch 2.14.1+cu126 and CUDA availability. Source `../gpu-env.sh` before GPU inference, media processing or tests, and use the existing `.venv`; do not replace its packages for an experiment. `supervision` was importable while `transformers`, `accelerate`, `torchreid` and `mmpose` were not; RTMPose may still be present through another runtime. The model registry identifies RF-DETR, RTMPose and TrackNet: verify the selected backend, files and checkpoint hashes before claiming they ran.

The 22 October court-isolation report measures **geometric leakage**, not labelled shuttle, activity, identity or event accuracy. The user confirmed no human-reviewed activity or identity labels exist. `research/evaluate_session.py` requires reviewed source-bound intervals for accuracy claims, and currently evaluates rallies rather than all requested activity and identity classes. A previously run focused regression suite passed 71 tests; that does not establish video accuracy. A bounded RF-DETR timing experiment is recorded below. Peak VRAM, long-video cache growth and before/after classification accuracy remain unmeasured.

## Proposed evidence flow

1. Preserve the existing court candidates and line geometry. Record quality, selected court and camera validity; allow bounded recovery under occlusion, invalidate after movement, and never silently switch courts.
2. Detect and track people on the selected court. Keep temporary track ID, hypothesised participant ID and user-assigned name distinct. Combine motion, time and spatial evidence with optional appearance; expose unresolved substitutions and re-entry.
3. Aggregate timestamped evidence from participant visibility, movement and pose, shuttle trajectory, credible contact cues and gaps. Separate observed exchange activity from inferred match membership. A wrong-side serve alone is not evidence of casual activity. Return unknown or review-needed when match play and practice are visually indistinguishable.
4. Use deterministic temporal rules, hysteresis, missing-data handling and provenance. An optional agent or vision-language reviewer may propose uncertain windows and explanations, but cannot overwrite geometry or authoritative events. Gate spatial statistics on valid geometry and match statistics on explicit eligibility; version policies for historical results.

## Efficient evaluation

Select short windows across distinct recordings, including the documented multicourt amateur footage. Sample sparsely for scene changes, then inspect denser frames and continuous clips around suspected entries, exits, substitutions, camera movements and rally boundaries. Record source hash, canonical timestamps, target court, visibility, uncertainty and reviewer provenance. Human-reviewed held-out examples are required for activity and identity accuracy, false inclusion and false exclusion claims; synthetic regressions and unreviewed model suggestions are diagnostics only.

For each controlled model trial record checkpoint hash, frame resolution, sampling cadence, software versions, per-process peak GPU memory, available VRAM, CPU/RAM, throughput, video length and bounded cache behaviour. Preserve existing jobs and evaluate geometry, identity, activity and downstream contamination on identical recordings and annotations.

## Task 1 continuation: safeguards and remaining gaps

The perception pipeline now records `VALID`, `DEGRADED`, `LOST`, or `REACQUIRING`, and requires consecutive verified main-court frames to regain validity. The camera hold counter persists across chunks. Codec v7 rejects older or incomplete cached observations; existing session results are not changed by this branch. Static or unverified calibration is degraded. Projected player positions and geometry-based winners are withheld where the relevant geometry is invalid. Alternate camera rows remain available for display but cannot be treated as the main camera.

`ParticipantTracker` emits provisional visual participant hypotheses distinct from the P1/P2 scoring slots and user-assigned names. Entry, exit and substitution events are hypotheses, not proven people crossing a court boundary. An exit counter advances only on frames where the detector actually ran with usable court coverage; this still does not establish perfect recall. Participants first seen at initialization are marked first-observed rather than entrants. Duplicate and unavailable track IDs do not establish absence. The tracker has bounded state and a deterministic session/frame epoch, but does not recover identity after a long disappearance or detect all tracker ID reuse. Restart uses a new epoch and requires reconciliation. Candidate confidence is the supporting detector/track score, not a calibrated identity probability.

When three or more distinct court tracks are observed, singles P1/P2 attribution and geometry-only winner assignment are withheld for the remainder of that analysis; raw unassigned visual tracks remain available. This conservative latch can exclude valid singles analytics after a passer-by enters and has no user reset workflow yet. It does not implement doubles team or scoring semantics.

The temporal activity layer combines bounded history of court-valid people, motion across a half-second window and shuttle observations. It labels supported active play, reacquiring, or unknown without claiming match intent. Missing shuttle detections alone cannot establish rest or conversation; the integration does not currently supply independently verified absence or rally evidence, so inactive and rally states remain unreachable here. Sparse shuttle detection can leave genuine activity unknown. Human match-start markers still do not automatically exclude casual play during later breaks.

These safeguards do not establish reviewed identity, event, or court accuracy. The final held-out labelled benchmark, robust re-identification and substitution validation, long-session profiling under varied conditions, all requested activity distinctions, and a user-review workflow remain open.

## Follow-up: provisional teams, sparse activity and reviewed intervals

The participant tracker now emits bounded, unconfirmed re-identification suggestions from a strong HSV similarity and image-position proximity, without merging participant IDs. These values are weak heuristic cues, not identity probabilities. They can confuse similar clothing, fail under lighting changes, and do not recover across process restart. No learned ReID model is enabled; the OSNet probe above demonstrates hardware feasibility only.

The doubles tracker proposes pairs only with four distinct participant hypotheses, finite court-relative positions away from the net, a stable 2+2 split and sustained observed active-play evidence. Team pairs are provisional and never mapped to P1/P2 scoring identities. The system withdraws a proposal on lost evidence or restart. Sparse shuttle observations plus sustained movement can support `active_play` in `temporal_activity_v2`; this is an uncalibrated observation hypothesis, not proof of rally, warm-up, practice or match intent.

For reviewed matches, `POST /api/sessions/{id}/corrections` supports `kind=match_intervals` with explicit inclusive frame pairs. Only complete rallies contained in a human-reviewed interval become eligible; unreviewed gaps and boundary-crossing rallies remain unofficial/unknown, and a per-rally review can override. `intervals: null` explicitly removes this policy; `[]` explicitly marks no match interval. Markers are human-verified events, restored and re-folded through the event log; no existing session was modified during this work. There is no dedicated UI for entering these intervals yet.

Two 600-frame unlabelled diagnostic probes before the identity-epoch safety change (`task1-v2-multi.json`, `task1-v2-second.json`) reported active-play hypotheses on 248 and 185 frames respectively, with 345 and 407 unknown frames; the remaining 7 and 8 frames were reacquiring. Neither probe produced a doubles team hypothesis. These are behaviour counts, not accuracy results, and provide no evidence that warm-up, casual practice, or match intent can be distinguished. Both recordings show similar settings. Human review and genuinely varied held-out cameras remain required.

Identity episodes are restarted on a shot/tracker reset, calibration-reference change, or more than two seconds without usable court coverage. A new episode does not inherit participant IDs or the in-memory ReID gallery. This avoids silently matching reused tracker IDs but can fragment a real participant. A session-level identity-ambiguity marker suppresses automatic singles assignment after such a break; human adjudication remains necessary. Brief coverage loss retains continuity. Reviewed match policy markers are restored by append sequence rather than video-frame position; reanalysis carry-over preserves the latest marker separately for match-start and reviewed intervals.

After the identity-epoch safety changes, both 600-frame probes were rerun using the unchanged source videos. Their activity counts remained 248/345/7 and 185/407/8 for active-play/unknown/reacquiring respectively, with no team hypotheses and no official points. They are unlabelled diagnostic observations, not evidence of correctness. Aggregated results and engine hashes are in `docs/evidence/task1-epoch-multi.json` and `task1-epoch-second.json`. The final badminton, engine and API regression suite passed 396 tests, with three existing Starlette deprecation warnings. Five local review clips and a blank review CSV were prepared outside the repository. Human-reviewed labels, camera diversity, learned ReID validation, reliable match-intent inference and full doubles scoring remain incomplete; do not claim Task 1 production readiness.
