# Task 2: individual participant review

The roster stores stable person IDs separately from the two legacy scoring slots. A doubles roster can name four people and their teams, but entering names or teams does not identify tracks or change scoring rules. A format explicitly chosen at session creation is applied to the rally in/out court geometry when its engine starts. A format changed after analysis starts takes effect on the next engine creation; existing events are not rewritten and require backed-up reanalysis before interpreting them as having used the new geometry.

The player-evidence workflow first shows the selected track's stored box on an exact video frame. A reviewer can then explicitly associate that one frame with a roster person, and separately confirm a shot's player at the same frame. The UI deliberately limits the interval to one frame because a tracker can switch people without an explicit reset. The identity review and shot-player review are independent, revocable records. A player review does not verify the predicted shot category.

Individual totals count only current unique stroke events referenced by official rallies with both live review records. Unreviewed events are unassigned; old unlinked rally shots are reported separately. Tracking episodes, proximity hypotheses, and left/right court sides do not themselves identify a person. The list of shot categories is still model-generated unless independently corrected.

Old recordings lack the necessary per-episode track-box provenance and cannot use the visual player review until they are reanalysed with this implementation. Reanalysis can change session results: back them up first as required by the project instructions. No existing session was reanalysed during this work. Setting a roster to doubles selects doubles court geometry but does not yet configure a complete doubles rules or identity model, nor does this feature provide independently validated four-player detection, full-match coverage, or high shot-classification accuracy. See `motion-probes/README.md` for bounded three-recording diagnostics and the missing ground truth.


A stroke-to-person review now also requires its provisional hypothesis to name the exact birth observation used by the identity review. Historical hypotheses without that field, or whose birth was replaced, remain unattributed even if their numeric track and episode IDs look the same. Back up the session before reanalysing to produce new provenance; previous review records are not silently transferred to new tracking episodes.

Generic event verification can correct a shot label, but cannot replace its tracker hypothesis. Older corrections that changed that hypothesis relative to the machine-origin stroke remain unattributed, even if the current fields appear to match a review.
