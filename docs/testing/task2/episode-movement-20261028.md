# Anonymous episode movement estimates

Historical validation record. The track movement feature and its API were removed on 2026-10-30; the behavior below describes the retired implementation.

`GET /api/sessions/{session_id}/episode-movement?from=START&to=END` accepts an inclusive canonical frame window of at most 120 seconds. It returns separate temporary track-episode summaries, not named players. Missing observations, calibration, valid camera poses or usable continuous movement produce `available: false` / `distance_m: null` rather than a fabricated zero. A successfully sampled stationary episode may instead have distance zero.

New doubles track samples retain court coordinates only when the episode birth, detector track and valid target-court projection agree. Existing samples lacking those coordinates or episode-birth provenance cannot support the metric; reanalysis must follow a verified session backup. The movement helper also requires one in-frame player box per track/frame, confidence at least 0.5, finite court points inside the bounded target court, and a unique visible main-camera matrix with quality at least 0.6 and condition number below 1e12. It stops an episode at reset, exit, reuse or relevant identity uncertainty. Samples are never joined across an observation ID, track ID, rejected frame or missing frame.

`valid_frames` counts individually accepted position samples in the requested window; `coverage_fraction` divides that number by all frames requested, including periods where the person was not visible. `observed_seconds` counts contiguous accepted segments that survive the movement policy; tracker output can contain model-predicted positions, so it is not necessarily independently detected time. `positions_m` contains lexicographically ordered, unordered court samples derived from the roughly 5 Hz movement policy; they carry no temporal order or segment boundaries and must never be connected as a path. The heuristic suppresses small jitter, rejects adjacent speeds over 12 m/s and does not interpolate gaps. Calibration error, tracker switches and pose/box jitter can still distort distance; these are uncalibrated provisional estimates, not validated biomechanical measurements, named-player distance, official-match totals or complete-match coverage.


Each episode also reports `detector_observed_frames`, `carried_frames`, and `unknown_source_frames` among its accepted `valid_frames`; these three counts sum to `valid_frames`. The first means the detector ran on that frame, not that the specific track was matched to a new detection. Carried means the person detector did not run; unknown covers legacy samples lacking the flag. Distance and `observed_seconds` remain provisional and can include carried or unknown-source samples; they are not independently detected distance or time. Human review's exact-frame track-box endpoint only exposes a box when the detector-run flag is explicitly true; false or missing provenance is unavailable and may require backed-up reanalysis. The identity-review POST independently requires such a unique, birth-bound track box at both requested interval endpoints. Existing human review records remain readable and revocable even when historical source flags are absent.

The stroke participant-review POST also requires a unique birth-bound, detector-run track box at the exact stroke frame. A reviewed interval's endpoint checks alone cannot establish evidence for an interior frame. This source flag records that the detector ran on the frame; it does not prove that the particular person box was matched by the detector.
