Badminton matches are played as the best of three games, with each game played to 21 points under the rally point scoring system, where a point is awarded on every serve regardless of who served. If the score reaches 20–20, a two-point lead is required to win, but at 29–29, the side scoring the 30th point wins. Every rally results in a point, and service changes hands accordingly. Players are allowed a 60-second interval when the leading score reaches 11 points in a game and a two-minute break between games. Players change ends after each game, and in the third game, they switch ends when the leading score reaches 11 points. Serves must be hit underhand, below the waist, diagonally across the court, with both feet stationary until the serve is delivered, and only when the receiver is ready. A fault occurs if the shuttle lands outside the boundaries, fails to cross the net, passes through or under it, touches the ceiling or player’s body/clothing, or if a player touches the net or invades the opponent’s court. Carrying or slinging the shuttle on the racket during a stroke is also a fault. If both sides commit faults simultaneously or if play is disrupted by unforeseen circumstances, a let is called. Matches are played on BWF-approved courts using approved rackets and shuttles, with tournaments following BWF rankings for draws and seedings. Umpires officiate matches with line judges assisting, and video reviews such as Hawk-Eye may be used if available. Medical time-outs and referee stoppages for injuries or interruptions follow BWF regulations. Players are expected to uphold a strict code of conduct, avoiding dangerous play, unsporting behavior, deliberate time-wasting, or abuse of officials, as such actions are not tolerated.
