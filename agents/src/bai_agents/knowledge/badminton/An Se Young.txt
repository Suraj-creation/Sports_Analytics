An Se-young, born on February 5, 2002, in Gwangju, South Korea, is one of the brightest stars in world badminton today and has quickly risen to the very top of the sport with her extraordinary talent, discipline, and mental toughness. From a young age, she showed exceptional promise, joining the South Korean national team in 2018 as its youngest ever member while still a middle school student. That same year, she won her first senior international title at the Irish Open, signaling the arrival of a new force in women’s singles badminton. In 2019, she truly broke through on the world stage, capturing her first BWF World Tour title at the New Zealand Open by defeating Olympic champion Li Xuerui. Over the course of the year, she went on to win multiple titles including the Canada Open, French Open, and Korea Masters, and by the end of the season she had entered the top ten of the world rankings, an extraordinary feat for a player still in her teens. Her ability to combine strong defensive skills with precise attacking shots made her stand out against much more experienced opponents.

The years that followed only cemented her reputation. Even during the difficult period of the COVID-19 pandemic, An Se-young continued to train and compete at the highest level. In 2021 she made history by winning the Indonesia Masters, Indonesia Open, and the BWF World Tour Finals in quick succession, achievements that confirmed her as one of the sport’s new superstars. In 2022, she played a key role in helping South Korea to victories in both the Uber Cup and the Asian Games women’s team event, while also adding titles such as the Korea Open, Malaysia Masters, and Australian Open to her growing collection. She also secured bronze medals at both the Asian Championships and the World Championships, showing that she was consistently competitive against the very best players in the world.

The year 2023 was perhaps the most defining of her career so far. In August, An became the first South Korean woman ever to win the BWF World Championships in women’s singles, defeating Spain’s Carolina Marin in the final. A few weeks later, she followed up with a historic triumph at the Asian Games in Hangzhou, winning gold in both the singles and team events. Her singles gold was particularly significant as it ended a 29-year drought for South Korea in that category. Over the course of the season she collected eight World Tour titles and lost only three matches, a level of dominance that earned her the BWF Female Player of the Year award. In 2024 she crowned her achievements with Olympic glory in Paris, where she won the women’s singles gold medal, becoming the first South Korean woman since 1996 to do so. Her triumph was widely celebrated at home and established her as a national icon. Continuing into 2025, she added the All England Open and several other major titles to her name, demonstrating that her consistency and hunger for success remain unmatched. At only twenty-three years old, An Se-young has already achieved what many players can only dream of, and she continues to inspire a new generation of badminton fans and athletes worldwide.

What makes An Se-young even more fascinating is her unique playing style, which combines patience, intelligence, and incredible defensive resilience. She is widely known for her ability to return almost every shot, forcing her opponents into long rallies where she uses her physical endurance and mental strength to outlast them. Her defensive skills are often described as some of the best in modern badminton, yet she is not purely a retriever—she mixes her defense with sudden bursts of attacking play, using sharp smashes, deceptive drop shots, and precise net play to seize control of points. Unlike some power-based players, her game is built on anticipation, footwork, and consistency, which allows her to stay calm even under pressure. Many commentators note that her style reflects the discipline and patience often associated with South Korean badminton, but with her own flair for tactical adjustments mid-match. This balanced approach of rock-solid defense blended with well-timed aggression has made her one of the hardest players to beat and explains her extraordinary consistency on the world stage.
